Decision principles

These principles describe the selected pilot's preferences. Combine them with the board and the printed card text; a preference is not a guarantee that a play is safe.

  • Prefer efficient actions when their outcomes are similar.

  • Giving the opponent more memory needs a strong payoff.

  • Use available attacks to pressure security.

  • At zero opposing security, look for an attack that can end the game; check blockers and counters first.

  • Attack vulnerable opposing Digimon when your attacker can survive.

  • Avoid battles that sacrifice your attacker without a clear benefit.

  • Digivolution develops your line and draws a card.

  • Grow a line in breeding while it is protected.

  • Advance your evolution line toward higher levels.

  • Value evolution that improves battle strength.

  • Build a board when the memory cost is reasonable.

  • Establish Tamers when you can afford the tempo investment.

  • Hatch when the breeding area is empty.

  • Move a developed line out to contest the board.

  • At zero security, prioritize blocking an attack on the player.

  • Consider optional effects and defensive responses when their costs are acceptable.

  • Finish optional selections when additional cards do not improve the outcome.

  • Keep an opening hand with a rookie that can start your line.

How this guide was selected

We evolved a general pilot on varied legal decks, then trained deck pilots against search and a frozen archive of other pilots. Final games were excluded from training and selection. Search samples unknown cards using an open deck list and applies to main choices.
